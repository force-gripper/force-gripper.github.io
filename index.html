Abstract

Delicate object grasping benefits from precise force regulation and rapid reactions to physical contact, which are difficult to achieve with conventional width-based gripper control and vision-only policies. In this work, we develop a low-cost force-controlled parallel-jaw gripper driven by a timing-belt transmission and equipped with tactile sensing at the fingertips. To support learning-based force control, we further design a custom teleoperation device that captures human-applied grasping force and directly maps it to the robot gripper, enabling the collection of force-aware demonstrations. Learning force control for grasping is challenging due to its contact-dependent and high-frequency. We therefore introduce RETAF (Reactive Tactile Adaptation of Force), a lightweight force policy that operates alongside a pose-prediction base policy. Using our gripper and data collection device, we evaluate RETAF on multiple delicate manipulation tasks, including tofu grasping, chip picking, and cherry tomato manipulation. Experimental results show that direct force control consistently outperforms width control, tactile sensing is essential for achieving stable grasps, and RETAF improves performance across different base pose-prediction policies. Together, our gripper design and reactive force control framework provide an effective hardware–policy combination for advancing learning-based delicate object grasping.
